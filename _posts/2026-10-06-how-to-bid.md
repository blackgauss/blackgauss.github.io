# How to Bid
*October 6, 2026*

Every production bidding system in the literature factorizes the bid the same
way:

```
bid(x) = multiplier · value(x),     value(x) = pCTR(x) · pCVR(x) · V
```

The `value` half is the ML problem — calibrated probabilities, covered
elsewhere. The interesting half is the *multiplier*: what the optimal
function of value is, given a budget, a CPA target, an auction format, and
a competitive landscape. What I find remarkable is that this half is
*solved* — the optimal shape is derivable once, and every constrained variant
reduces to the same structural theorem with more dual numbers.

## Linear is only the unconstrained special case

The production heuristic for years was `b = b₀·θ/θ̄` — bid proportional to the
instance pCTR over the segment average (Zhang et al. 2016). Linear in value.
The central result of the bidding literature: linear-in-value is optimal
*only when no constraint binds*. And bidding your true value is only dominant
in the same frictionless case — with budgets and repeated interaction,
Balseiro, Besbes and Weintraub proved (*Management Science*, 2015),
truthfulness dies. Constraints force nonlinearity, and the nonlinearity is
computable.

## ORB: the KKT derivation

Zhang, Yuan and Wang (KDD 2014), *Optimal Real-Time Bidding*: maximize clicks
over a bidding *function* `b(θ)`, subject to expected spend ≤ budget B. Write
the LP over the KPI density with `w(b)` the landscape win-rate, dualize with
one multiplier λ for the budget, take the Euler–Lagrange condition w.r.t.
`b(θ)`, and it collapses to:

```
λ·w(b) = (θ − λ·b)·w′(b)
```

The KPI density `pθ(θ)` *cancels*. The optimal bid depends only on the
winning function and λ. Value distribution sets λ; the landscape sets the
*shape*. Two parametric landscapes, two closed forms:

- `w(b) = b/(c+b)` (concave; fits most markets) gives `b* = √(cθ/λ + c²) − c`.
- `w(b) = b²/(c²+b²)` (S-shaped; high-reserve markets) gives a Cardano
  difference-of-cube-roots.

Both are concave in θ: the optimal bidder bid *more than linearly* on cheap
traffic and *less* on expensive — buy many cheap impressions, not few
expensive ones, because marginal win-rate per cent is highest at low bids.
λ itself is set by the budget-feasibility root-finding (bisection on a
monotone spend function). Note the decomposition: value model from ML, shape
from the landscape, level from the budget. Three subsystems, one equation.

## USCB: everything is one bid function

He et al. (Alibaba, KDD 2021) take the LP with M side constraints — budget,
CPA/ROI/CPC targets, deep-conversion ratios — dualize all of them, and by
complementary slackness land on a single optimal-bid theorem:

```
b* = w₀·v − Σⱼ wⱼ·( qⱼ(1 − cⱼ) − kⱼ·pⱼ )
```

Structure: *linear value scaling minus per-constraint correction terms.* A
cost-related constraint (CPA, ROI) contributes no additive correction — it
just inflates the denominator, i.e. acts as a pure shading multiplier. That's
a lovely one-liner: **a CPA constraint is a tax on the multiplier, not a
filter on impressions.** All the multi-constraint bookkeeping reduces to K+1
dual numbers, solved offline with a real LP solver or online by dual ascent.

## The online solvers

λ must be solved *while the auctions run*, since supply is unknown. The map
λ→spend has unknown, drifting gain, and the literature's answer converges on
feedback control: PID on log-bid (waterlevel = integrator-only PID, robust to
delayed feedback; D-terms amplify noise, P-only leaves offset — this is
textbook control showing up verbatim inside adtech, Zhang et al. WSDM 2016 and
LinkedIn's waterlevel pacer KDD 2014), or the modern canonical form,
multiplicative dual descent `λ ← λ·exp(η·(c − B/T)) / Z`, which has regret
guarantees (Õ(√T)), provably *no early depletion* (λ rises before the budget
does — Alibaba/Tencent's pacing book's mirror-descent treatment), and per the
theory literature *subsumes PID* as its log-space linear-reference special
case. When all bidders run these loops, there's still an equilibrium — the
maximal budget-feasible multiplier vector, unique and Pareto-dominant — and
when pacing equilibria are PPAD-hard to compute (Chen, Kroer, Kumar, *Math.
OR* 2023), which is exactly why nobody computes the fixed point and instead
everyone runs feedback.

## And then RL

The RL formulations (DDQN bidding, MDPs over auction states) are honest but
carry a caveat worth internalizing: the environment is partly *you* — your
own pacing changes the state distribution — and offline logs are
selection-biased by your past policy. Production papers that win (USCB above)
are characterized by simple policy class, many dual variables, and a robust
online solver, rather than by policy complexity. The bid function should be
derivable, not just learned.
